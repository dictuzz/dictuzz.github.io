Governance Playbook

A fictional governance system designed to make delegation, reporting lines, and team rituals feel clear without feeling rigid.

Role Governance designer and meeting architect
Scope Decision rights, meeting rhythm, responsibility mapping
Team size 34 active contributors across six functional pods
Impact 39% fewer unresolved blockers after four weeks

The brief

A fast-growing team had energy, but the operating model was inconsistent. Important decisions were getting revisited, responsibilities blurred during execution, and follow-up quality depended too heavily on individual discipline.

The challenge

Governance fails when it reads like bureaucracy. The system needed to create structure that people would actually use, especially under deadline pressure.

The approach

I built a playbook around three elements: clear decision ownership, one ritual for escalation, and meeting templates that ended with explicit next actions. The goal was to reduce ambiguity without creating extra admin work.

What changed

Teams stopped using meetings as discovery sessions and started using them as commitment moments. Coordination improved because everyone could see where authority started, where escalation belonged, and what “done” looked like after each checkpoint.
