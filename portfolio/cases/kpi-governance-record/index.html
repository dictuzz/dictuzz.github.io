KPI-Based Governance

Led 34 members across 10 divisions through a structured KPI rhythm — a council that learned to operate with more maturity, accountability, and visible momentum.

By Benedictus Richard Ricardo President · SMA Santa Maria 2 Nov 2021 – Nov 2022 · Jakarta, ID

A large student organization needed stronger rhythm, accountability, and program structure. I introduced a KPI-based governance approach to help leadership and execution operate with more clarity.

A 34-member council spread across 10 divisions needed better visibility, more consistent follow-through, and a clearer way to evaluate how programs were progressing. The challenge was scale: once a team becomes multi-division, informal leadership stops being enough. Without structure, momentum becomes uneven and accountability starts to blur.

What I changed

I introduced a more explicit governance model with measurable expectations, recurring checkpoints, and better alignment between division work and institution-wide objectives. The organization moved to a KPI-based operating rhythm, with weekly reviews, clearer division expectations, and a stronger institutional planning approach for larger school programs.

A council that could finally see what was moving, what was blocked, and where leadership energy needed to be applied.

Why it mattered

The result was a council that could operate with more maturity. It became easier to see what was moving, what was blocked, and where leadership energy needed to be applied. Programs ran with more stability, both internally and outward — including campaigns that engaged 12 feeder schools — because the operating cadence held under load.
